Harvey’s footsteps

The plants of the Saanich Peninsula have been known, named and cared for by many people. The W̱SÁNEĆ people have known them for countless generations, and their relationships with these plants and places continue today.

The earliest botanical collection represented in this atlas dates to 1859. Since then, generations of botanists and collectors, professionals and amateurs, neighbours and naturalists have added their observations. Some recorded the names of plants and the places where they grew, though much more knowledge has been carried on in memory and practice, which remains vital to the ongoing care of these lands.

This project is dedicated to the memory of Harvey Janszen (July 11, 1946–May 10, 2021) and fellow naturalists, including Hans Roemer, who have passed away in recent years. Harvey spent decades getting to know the plants of the Salish Sea. He collected more than 3,000 vascular plant specimens, kept field journals from 1973 to 2017, and compiled species checklists for the southern Gulf Islands, the Saanich Peninsula, and the San Juan Islands. This project is an invitation to help carry this work forward, by sharing what we’ve learned and enlisting others in the creation of a living flora for the Saanich Peninsula.

This project is at once in memory of the plants themselves. Each record marks a moment when someone stopped to notice a plant in its place. Some of those plants are still waiting to be found again. Some may never be.
